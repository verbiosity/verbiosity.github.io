Cloud

We have some serious issues of the "everything is wrong" variety and have entrusted decision-making to the everything-is-wrong machine, what could go even righter?

I read some toot saying that AI is not the problem, the structures of how things are is the problem. It gives a long list of things that are wrong and rather depressing, and who is to deny the reality of that? The toot flew by. Half my bloody timeline is like an extended George Monbiot article about how terrible things are in the AI era. I try, and probably fail not to talk about it too much. The Weaver's Guild bringing each other down, when we could be out smashing looms, metaphorically speaking. We could be symbolically on strike.

The toot is not even right enough. A lot of what we find so anathema (is that the right context? I don't want to get sucked into an explanation by looking it up) is not AI-in-everything, it's where everything is. Hyperscale! A handful of greedy, behavioural data mining companies - companies isn't the right word, is it - entities? running and managing the information infrastructure that everyone has come to depend on.

The datacentre boom is a jockeying for position. AI may be the driver, but the compute, the storage, it could be anything.

The institutional dynamic that says that if you're at a certain scale, you can't self-manage. A whole generation of millenial IT managers who have never known things to be any other way. You buy the cloud, AWS or Microsoft or Google are a sunk cost of doing business.

We lowered our resistance - as a profession, as a society. Central mandates tell us to do things in a certain way that only externally-managed, Cloud-based infrastructure can be seen to support. My god, i'm starting to sound like a George Monbiot article. Did i mention that i had diarrhea?

And maybe the cost of it all will become too much. I mean the financial cost here - the human cost and the environmental cost aren't even factored into the equation. Maybe the 2000-meets-2008-style crash, when it eventually comes (and the longer it's artificially restrained, the harder it'll eventually hit), will fall through the property and energy speculation business that provides a kind of under-drive to AI mania.

But I don't think we can wait around for that to happen. Personally, i never planned for a pension in a structured way, never took out a mortgage with a bank, not even a credit card. The underlying belief there was that the global financial system would surely have collapsed by the time i reached pensionable age. But it still hasn't, and now i'm less than 8 years from a state-sponsored free bus pass and questioning the quality of this decision.

We can't wait around for it to burn. I've known a few radio-tech resilience folks over the years, hams, mesh networks. Street-level, city-level data centres at the size of a stair cupboard or a shed. Ways of preserving the good parts of connectedness and automation without the dependency on the globally harnessed lying machine operated by a handful of whatever oligarchs are now, I'd look it up but don't want to risk putting myself in the way of another explain
