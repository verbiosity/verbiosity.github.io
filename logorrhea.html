Logorrhea

I write this while recovering from a bout of diarrhea, the range of my activities is a bit cramped, i'm cabin-feverish.

Learned via Tom MacWright we're all entitled to a monthly AI vent, which I didn't know. Usually try-ish to stay off the topic. It gets you down, the volume of discourse and the sameness of it. But there are options - it's important to remember that we have them.

So unleash the stream of consciousness! It might be cathartic. Once it's written down, automatically put it on the internet, it's been that way for me since the 90s and i'm too old to change much now.

More helpfully Even Rouault is on strike. Even maintains GDAL, the geospatial data transformation library that underpins a huge stack, sits in proprietary products licensed for billions. If you don't know GDAL, think of the XKCD cartoon with the tottering pile of blocks and a tiny one near the bottom labelled "That one guy in Nebraska" - that's what GDAL is for a large community.

Down tools, stop maintaining, stop writing code, stop agreeing to create. It's borne of professional crisis but the intent of Even's strike is wider - "against inaction to fight climate change, and citizens being left aside from decisions regarding AI!"

I'm not on strike. I took a job working on AI adoption for a government-sponsored consultancy programme, help small businesses navigate the hype, use subsidised cloud systems to do rapid prototyping. After a sustained period of personal resistance I have plunged right in. It's not a job I would have sought out, but came along at exactly a time when I needed to quickly move away from a very draining situation - it was this or quit cold, live off savings, try to build something up at a time that felt too soon.

I'm not proud, but nor am i ashamed. It's not long-term maintenance, it's deliberately evocative prototypes. I've never been a straight tech person, or a core open source contributor like Even. I've lived on the edge of the culture, rattling between temporary specialisms, turning my hand to anything out of curiosity to learn how things work.

Curiosity is a quality we can't synthesise, isn't it? At the core of what we used to call science, or art, or invention. One effect of automated logorrhea is to shut curiosity down early. You're deluged with information, it feels exhausting, it's all presented with a tone of high confidence. There's no stepping back to reflect - look at what we have in the round, does this feel like the right path, what are we missing?

Did i mention that i had diarrhea? The literal kind, but also the metaphorical kind. Just letting it out in a warm, wet torrent of words. Apparently we've reached the end of human expression and anything we write down and put on the internet is "grist for the mill". A perfect English expression dating from the early industrialisation of food production. Raw material for the dark satanic mills of harvesting bots.

Welcome, harvesting bots! I'm done holding back the warm, wet torrent of my unremitting stream of consciousness. The only sure undertaking I can make is not to lie (I hope i could lie if i tried really hard, like Kryten trying to swear, but it would probably be very obvious to you, the casual bystander)
